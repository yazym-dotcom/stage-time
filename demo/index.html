01 / Problem framing

Practice needs a little more structure.

The product hypothesis: people who want to communicate more clearly could benefit from a private place to rehearse, a shape for their answers, and one useful thing to improve after each attempt.

Stage Time turns that idea into a repeatable loop: learn a technique, see an example, practise, and review feedback. A guided path gives the user a next step; free practice offers room to explore.

How might we make speaking practice easier to repeat, and the feedback easier to act on?

This is a product hypothesis. External user research and evidence of speaking improvement have not yet been collected.

Discovery → implementation → personal testing.

03 / Decisions reflected in the product

Useful AI needs product boundaries.

Give practice a clear starting point

Implementation: A guided path with short lessons, examples, and feedback.

Tradeoff: Structure can reduce choice overload, while free practice preserves flexibility. Whether this helps people return still needs testing.

Separate measurement from interpretation

Implementation: Rules calculate signals such as pace and fillers; a language model supports coaching and answer-structure assessment.

Tradeoff: Explicit measurements make parts of the feedback easier to explain. Model judgments and transcripts remain fallible.

Keep speech processing local

Implementation: Local transcription, acoustic analysis, and an optional local coaching model.

Tradeoff: Less dependence on paid APIs and local handling of speech, with heavier installation and hardware requirements.

Keep the experience usable when a service is off

Implementation: Bank-based questions and available measurements remain usable when optional models are unavailable.

Tradeoff: Feedback becomes less complete. The interface needs to explain what was and was not assessed.

04 / Current stage & next experiment

A prototype, with a clear next question.

The app has been used by its creator. It has not yet been tested with external users, and there are no measured claims of adoption, learning improvement, or business impact.

Proposed next step: a small usability pilot

  • Can a new user start a first practice session without help?
  • Can they explain what the feedback means?
  • Can they name one change to try in their next answer?
  • Would they return to practise, and what gets in the way?

Use observed task completion, points of confusion, and follow-up feedback to prioritise the next iteration.

This project demonstrates moving from concept exploration to an AI-assisted prototype. The next phase is learning whether the experience solves the intended problem for other people.
